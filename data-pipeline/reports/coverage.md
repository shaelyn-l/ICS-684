# Sample coverage by time period

Generated by `scripts/04_coverage.py`. Counts are the samples Step 1.5 will use, after the rules in `data_notes.md` (dates trimmed to 2004-12-31, blank infection results excluded, `U` infection status excluded, each bird counted once per site-period, station 200 dropped, NOBI events kept).

Period options:

- **Month**: e.g. `2002-03`.
- **Hawaiian season**: wet Nov–Apr, dry May–Oct. Wet seasons are labeled by start year (`2002-wet` = Nov 2002–Apr 2003).
- **Four seasons**: DJF, MAM, JJA, SON. December counts toward the next year's DJF.
- **Year**.

A period marked *partial* is cut short by the start or end date (for example `2000-wet` holds only Jan–Apr 2001, and `2005-DJF` holds only Dec 2004). Partial periods are included in the cell counts below.

## Data notes from this script

- Mosquito captures: 72 rows repeat another row's site, station, transect, date and trap type (usually with different catches, so probably a second trap). Each row is counted as one trap night.
- Bird blood samples: 684 rows have band number `NB` (no band); 27 of them have a determined status. Unbanded birds can't be matched across samples, so each `NB` row is counted as a separate bird.
- Bird blood samples: 8 band numbers appear under more than one species. Birds are counted as unique site + period + band number + species, so these are not merged.

# Recommendation

These are suggestions with trade-offs; the decision is yours. Record it in the Decisions log in PLAN.md and in data_notes.md. Figures below are for a 2002 start unless noted.

## (a) Period option: Hawaiian season (wet/dry)

- **Month** is too fine. Only 20% of site-months have ≥20 honeycreepers sampled and 28% have ≥20 mosquitoes examined, and 63% of site-months have no bird counts at all, because counts were done in rounds about every three months.
- **Four seasons** splits the bird count rounds badly: the rounds fall around Feb–Mar, May–Jun, Aug–Sep and Nov–Dec, which straddle the DJF/MAM and SON/DJF boundaries, so 28% of site-seasons have no bird counts while others get two rounds. Only 50% of cells have ≥20 honeycreepers sampled and 53% have ≥20 mosquitoes examined.
- **Hawaiian season** puts two count rounds in every site-season (0% zero cells), and raises the share of cells with ≥20 samples to 65% for honeycreepers and 68% for mosquitoes examined. Wet vs. dry is also a natural frame for mosquito breeding. Trade-offs: only 5 full seasons in 2002–2004 (7 slider steps counting the partial ones below), and each wet season spans two calendar years (`2002-wet` = Nov 2002–Apr 2003), which needs a sentence of explanation.
- **Year** has the most samples per cell but only three steps, and it hides the seasonal swing in mosquitoes and transmission.

With a 2002 start, the Hawaiian-season slider has two partial periods: `2001-wet` (Jan–Apr 2002 only; mosquito trapping starts in Feb and counts in Mar) and `2004-wet` (Nov–Dec 2004 only). Either keep them and label them as partial, or run the slider `2002-dry` to `2004-dry` (5 full seasons) and leave those months out.

## (b) Start date: 2002

- Mosquito captures begin in Feb 2002 and bird counts in Mar 2002, so a 2002 start is the first point where all four datasets are running. With a 2001 start, every 2001 period has zero trap nights and zero bird counts, so half the measures would be blank for those steps (Hawaiian season: 26% of cells with no trap nights vs. 5% with a 2002 start).
- Trade-off: a 2002 start drops the 2001 mosquito dissections and blood samples.

## (c) Minimum sample size for showing a percentage: 20

- At 20 samples one infected bird or mosquito moves the percentage by 5 points; the margin of error is still wide (roughly ±20 points near 50%), so pair it with the sample size in the popup.
- **10** shows more cells but lets a single sample swing the value by 10 points. **30** is steadier but blanks noticeably more cells (see the ≥30 columns).
- No minimum rescues mosquito infection at the two highest sites: SOL had 2 *Culex* caught in three years and CJR 76, so their infection percentages will be blank under any option. That is a real result (almost no mosquitoes at high elevation), so the app should say "too few mosquitoes to test" rather than show nothing.
- The minimum applies to percentages only (infection and prevalence). Abundance measures (per trap night, per count) can be shown whenever the sample size is above zero.
